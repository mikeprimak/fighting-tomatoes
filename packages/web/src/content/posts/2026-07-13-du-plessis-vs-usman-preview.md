Two former champions, both looking to prove they still belong at the top, meet in the main event when the UFC returns to Oklahoma City for the first time in nearly a decade. **UFC Fight Night: Du Plessis vs Usman takes place on Saturday, July 18, 2026** at the Paycom Center, headlined by former middleweight champion Dricus du Plessis against former welterweight king Kamaru Usman at 185 pounds. Here is the full card, the odds, start times, how to watch, and our honest read on the main event. *You can rate how hyped you are for every fight on this card on [Good Fights](https://goodfights.app).*

## When is Du Plessis vs Usman?

The fight is on **Saturday, July 18, 2026**, headlining a UFC Fight Night from Oklahoma City. It marks the promotion's first trip to OKC in almost ten years, and it brings a genuine main event with real stakes for two of the biggest names of the last several years.

## Where is the fight? What time does it start?

The event is at the **Paycom Center in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma**. The prelims begin at **5 p.m. ET / 2 p.m. PT**, and the main card follows at **8 p.m. ET / 5 p.m. PT**, with the Du Plessis vs Usman walkouts closing the night as the main event.

## How do I watch Du Plessis vs Usman?

In the United States, the entire card streams on **Paramount+**, prelims and main card alike, included with a subscription. There is no separate pay-per-view fee for a Fight Night. A single Paramount+ plan, around $8.99 per month for the Essential tier, gets you the whole night.

## What are the betting odds for Du Plessis vs Usman?

Dricus du Plessis is the favorite, around **-270**, with Kamaru Usman the underdog near **+220**. That is roughly a 73% implied chance for du Plessis, which reflects both his size at the natural weight and the fact that Usman is moving up a division to take the fight. In the co-main, Christian Leroy Duncan is favored over the veteran Jared Cannonier. Odds move all week, so treat these as a snapshot.

## Why is this a big fight?

Because it is a collision of two men who not long ago sat on top of the sport. Dricus du Plessis is a former middleweight champion coming off the loss of his belt, fighting for the first time since, and he needs a statement win to force his way back into the title picture. Kamaru Usman is a former welterweight champion and one of the most dominant fighters of his era, now jumping up to middleweight and chasing the two-division history that would cement his legacy. Two former kings, both with something to prove, is exactly the kind of main event that carries a Fight Night.

## Who is Dricus du Plessis?

Du Plessis is the awkward, relentless South African who ground his way to the middleweight title with a style nobody enjoys facing: unorthodox, high-pressure, and impossible to time. He is 23-3, and even in defeat he has been in the championship mix for years. This is his first fight since losing the belt, and he is the naturally bigger man at 185, which is a big part of why the books favor him here.

## Who is Kamaru Usman?

Usman, 21-4, is one of the greatest welterweights in UFC history, a former champion with a long title reign built on smothering wrestling and steadily improving boxing. Moving up to middleweight is a real gamble at this stage of his career, but a win over a former champion in his new division would put a two-division story back on the table and prove he still belongs at the very top. The size and the layoff are the questions; the pedigree is not.

## Who wins Du Plessis vs Usman? Our prediction

This is a fascinating stylistic puzzle. Usman's wrestling is the great equalizer, but du Plessis is bigger, awkward, and notoriously hard to hold down, and he pushes a pace that has broken more polished fighters. Usman's path is to make it a grappling fight and lean on his championship-level control. Du Plessis's path is to turn it into the kind of scrappy, high-volume brawl where his unorthodox pressure overwhelms.

**Prediction: du Plessis by decision or late stoppage, with Usman's wrestling keeping it competitive if he can consistently get it to the mat.** The size and activity edge lean toward the South African, but a prime-form Usman is never a safe fade.
