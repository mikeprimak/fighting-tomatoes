Conor McGregor came back after five years away, and it lasted 69 seconds. At **UFC 329 on Saturday, July 11, 2026** at T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas, McGregor blew out his right knee on the opening exchange of his comeback and could not continue, handing Max Holloway a first-round TKO and ending the biggest comeback story the sport had to offer almost before it began. Here is what happened and what it means, followed by the full preview we published before the fight. *You can rate this fight, and every fight on the card, on [Good Fights](https://goodfights.app).*

## Who won McGregor vs Holloway 2?

**Max Holloway won by first-round TKO, but not in any way anyone wanted.** McGregor came flying out of the gate with a switch kick in the opening seconds, landed it, and landed awkwardly on his right leg. The knee buckled underneath him. His balance was instantly gone, and he slipped to the canvas twice. To his credit, Holloway did not tee off on a hurt man, instead looking to referee Mike Beltran to ask whether to step in. After a brief restart and a single Holloway kick, McGregor could not put weight on the leg, and Beltran waved it off at **1:09 of the first round**. McGregor walked out of the cage under his own power but visibly limping, and UFC president Dana White said afterward that doctors suspected a **torn ACL**.

It is a cruel echo of history. McGregor has now suffered three catastrophic leg injuries in the defining fights of his career: he tore his ACL during the very first Holloway fight back in 2013 and gutted out the decision anyway, he snapped his leg against Dustin Poirier in 2021, and now the knee gave out on the opening exchange of his return. The one constant in the McGregor story of the last decade has become his own body failing at the worst moment.

## The most hyped fight we ever tracked, and one of the lowest-rated

Here is the number that captures the whole night. On Good Fights, McGregor vs Holloway 2 carried a pre-fight **hype score of 9.5 out of 10**, one of the highest we have ever recorded for any fight. The entire fan base was electric for it. Then the injury ended it, and the post-fight rating collapsed to roughly **1.3 out of 10**, one of the lowest scores on the platform. That gap between anticipation and payoff is the largest we have seen, and it is exactly how the McGregor faithful felt walking out: a fight they had dreamed about for months was over in 69 seconds without a meaningful punch landed.

## What did we predict, and how did it hold up?

We picked Holloway, and we said so plainly: the smart money was on him, and a five-year layoff, a surgically repaired leg, and the wrong stylistic matchup against a fighter at the peak of his craft was a very hard ask even for the greatest draw the sport has ever had. Our exact call was Holloway "by late stoppage or decision, with a genuine asterisk for McGregor's power early."

In the end the prediction is almost beside the point, because the fight never got to play out. Holloway's name is on the result, but he won it because McGregor's knee gave out on the opening exchange, not because either man's game plan came together. We spent months breaking down a stylistic puzzle that got 69 seconds to breathe. Nobody's read on this fight really got tested.

## What is next for McGregor and Holloway?

For McGregor, the question is bigger than any single fight. This was the third catastrophic leg injury of his career, he is 37, and he now faces another long rehab from a suspected torn ACL. He has one fight left on his current UFC contract, reportedly targeted for **April 2027**, but that timeline now depends entirely on the knee and the recovery. Before anyone talks about what is next, he has to get healthy and decide he still wants this at all.

For Holloway, the win goes on his record and he handled a strange, ugly moment with real class, but he did not get the signature performance he came for either. He is still one of the best and most beloved fighters alive, with bigger and fresher options waiting at welterweight and beyond. As for a **McGregor vs Holloway 3**, the injury ending paradoxically keeps a crack in the door that a clean loss would have shut, since nothing was actually settled in the cage. But a trilogy is now a hypothetical hanging on a 37-year-old's recovery, not a fight anyone can book today.

## The preview we wrote before UFC 329

Everything below is the preview we published in the weeks before the fight, kept here for the record. It is written from before the night, when this was still the most anticipated comeback in years and nobody knew it would end on a freak injury. The history, the tale of the tape, and the odds all still stand.

## Did McGregor and Holloway fight before? Who won?

Yes. They fought once before, more than a decade ago, and **Conor McGregor won by unanimous decision**. The two met on August 17, 2013 at a UFC Fight Night card in Boston, early in both of their careers. McGregor was a brash Irish prospect the American audience was just getting to know. Holloway was a 21-year-old up-and-comer. McGregor controlled the fight and took a clear decision on all three judges' scorecards.

## What happened in the first McGregor vs Holloway fight?

Here is the wild part, and the detail that made this rematch so strange even before UFC 329 added a new chapter: McGregor beat Holloway while **tearing his ACL during the fight**. He hurt the knee partway through, switched to a more measured, control-heavy approach to protect it, and still won every round on the way to a decision. He then needed surgery and a long layoff afterward. So the only completed meeting between these two saw a half-injured McGregor outpoint a barely-formed version of the man Holloway would become. That was never a result you could lean on too heavily 13 years later.

## How have McGregor and Holloway changed since 2013?

Completely. In 2013 they were both prospects. Since then, McGregor became a two-division UFC champion, the face of the sport, and the biggest pay-per-view draw in its history, then spent years split between fighting, boxing, business, and injury recovery. Holloway went on an entirely different trajectory: he became one of the greatest featherweights of all time, set the UFC record for significant strikes landed, captured the BMF title with a legendary buzzer-beater knockout of Justin Gaethje, and built a reputation as one of the toughest, highest-output fighters the sport has ever seen. The kid McGregor beat does not exist anymore.

## Why were McGregor and Holloway fighting again?

The rematch was built on star power, history, and timing. McGregor wanted a marquee return that the whole world would watch, and a do-over against a now-elite Holloway was exactly that kind of story. For the UFC, putting McGregor's comeback in the International Fight Week main event was the biggest possible draw for its biggest weekend. The 13-year gap between fights, plus everything both men had become since, gave the promotion a narrative that sold itself.

## What weight class was McGregor vs Holloway 2? Was it a title fight?

McGregor vs Holloway 2 was a **welterweight bout (170 pounds)**, and a **non-title fight**. There was no belt on the line, including no BMF title. That detail mattered more than it looked. Both men made their names in lighter divisions, McGregor at featherweight and lightweight, Holloway at featherweight, so meeting at 170 pounds was a notable choice that favored the bigger, fresher version of each fighter rather than the one grinding through a hard weight cut.

## How long had Conor McGregor been out of action?

A long time. McGregor's previous fight was in **July 2021**, when he suffered a gruesome broken leg at the end of the first round of his trilogy bout with Dustin Poirier. That meant he stepped into the Octagon at UFC 329 after roughly **five years away from competition**. Ring rust, the leg, his age, and the quality of the opponent were all reasons this was a far harder comeback than a star return usually looks on paper. In hindsight, the layoff and the surgically rebuilt lower body proved even more decisive than anyone feared.

One telling pattern in the betting: the public piled onto McGregor, with some books reporting over 90 percent of individual tickets on the Irishman, but the large, sharp wagers were overwhelmingly on Holloway.

## Why was McGregor such a big underdog?

A few reasons stacked up against him. He was the older fighter, coming off a five-year layoff and a serious leg injury, and facing an opponent who had stayed elite and active the entire time. Holloway's pressure, volume, and chin were a nightmare matchup for a fighter who relies on timing and explosive early offense, especially one who had not been tested in years. Oddsmakers were not betting against McGregor's talent. They were betting against the layoff, the age, and the specific style he had to solve.

## How did each man need to win?

**McGregor's path** was the one it had always been: land the left hand early. He is at his most dangerous in the first round, when he is fresh, sharp, and hunting for the counter left straight. If he could catch Holloway clean before the fight settled into deep waters, his power was more than enough to end any night. There was a tactical version too: in his 2016 rematch with Nate Diaz he boxed patiently, managed his output, and dug in for a hard decision, and a disciplined McGregor who picked his moments was always a tougher problem than a one-round gambler.

**Holloway's path** was to make it a long fight. His volume, footwork, and relentless output tend to break opponents down over five rounds, and he has the cardio and chin to wade through early power and take over. If he weathered the opening exchanges and dragged McGregor into the championship rounds, the numbers and the history of late-career McGregor both pointed his way. In the end, neither plan got tested: McGregor's opening kick decided the night before any of it could happen.

## Was McGregor's comeback clean? The drug-testing question

This became one of the bigger storylines of the comeback, so it is worth addressing head-on. McGregor was back in the UFC's anti-doping testing pool and, by the public test history, had been one of the most-tested fighters in the entire promotion to start 2026, which is exactly what you would expect for a returning star the UFC wants clean results on record for. His path back was not simple: he served an 18-month sanction for missing three attempted sample collections in 2024, and that suspension lapsed on March 20, 2026, clearing him to compete again.

The scrutiny got sharper in June 2026 when The New York Times published an investigation reporting that, citing people with direct knowledge, McGregor used banned substances during the long rehab from his 2021 broken leg, a period when he was out of competition and outside the testing pool. McGregor's surgeon denied prescribing steroids or hormones, and the broader defense has been that many "banned" substances have legitimate medical uses under therapeutic-use rules. Nothing in the report concerned a fight or an in-competition test. Still, it was fair context for fans weighing a comeback this size.

Enormous, and the ticket market proved it. McGregor's return turned UFC 329 into one of the hottest tickets in combat sports. When the UFC Fight Club presale went live in late May 2026, it sold out in minutes across both early-access windows. McGregor's own reaction to the instant sellout was a single laughing emoji.
